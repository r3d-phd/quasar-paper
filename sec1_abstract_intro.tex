\begin{abstract}
Controlling silicon spin qubits with machine-learning agents requires
physics-grounded simulation that faithfully reproduces device-specific
decoherence. Existing frameworks use abstract depolarising noise or
target a single platform, leaving a gap for cross-technology benchmarking.
We present \textbf{SiliQun~v5}, an open-source Lindblad density-matrix
platform with calibrated profiles for SiMOS quantum dots, phosphorus-donor
electron and nuclear spin qubits, and gate-all-around (GAA) nanowire
qubits. Each profile encodes $T_1$, $T_2$, charge noise, exchange noise,
and hyperfine coupling from peer-reviewed experiments, exposed through a
Gymnasium-compatible interface validated against Rabi, $T_1$-decay, and
Bell-state benchmarks.
Two independent replications confirm accuracy: DQL/SARSA on SiMOS
achieves $F = 0.9997$ ($\Delta F < 0.03\%$ vs.~published target); DQN
universal state preparation on the donor profile achieves
$\bar{F} = 0.9748 \pm 0.0098$. We report, to our knowledge, the first
machine-learning result on a GAA silicon spin qubit ($\bar{F} = 0.9877$).
Available as a pip-installable MIT-licensed package with a Qiskit-compatible interface, SiliQun enables reproducible cross-technology benchmarking on a unified simulation substrate.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

\subsection{The Need for a Multi-Technology Silicon Spin Qubit Simulator}
\label{subsec:motivation}

Silicon spin qubits have emerged as a leading candidate for scalable
quantum computing, combining long coherence times, compatibility with
standard CMOS fabrication, and a dense qubit topology.
Three competing silicon spin qubit architectures dominate current research efforts: metal-oxide-semiconductor (SiMOS) quantum dots~\cite{Xue2022,
Noiri2022,Philips2022}, phosphorus-donor (P:Si) electron and nuclear spin
qubits~\cite{Madzik2022,Morello2020}, and gate-all-around (GAA) nanowire
spin qubits~\cite{Tanamoto2025}.
Each technology occupies a distinct point in the coherence-time versus
fabrication-complexity trade-off space: SiMOS offers fast gate times
($\sim$1\,ns) with moderate coherence ($T_2 \sim 0.5$\,ms); donor qubits
offer exceptional coherence ($T_2 \sim 1$--28\,ms~\cite{Muhonen2014,Wolfowicz2013} — spanning electron spin ($T_2 \sim 1$\,ms) to nuclear spin ($T_2 \sim 28$\,ms) under dynamical decoupling) at the cost of slower
gates ($\sim$1\,$\mu$s) and placement precision requirements; GAA qubits
offer CMOS-foundry compatibility with coherence properties intermediate
between SiMOS and donor devices~\cite{Tanamoto2025}.

Despite this technological diversity, machine-learning studies treat
silicon spin qubits monolithically, overlooking the distinct noise regimes of each platform.
Previous machine-learning studies for silicon spin qubit
control concentrate disproportionately on SiMOS quantum dot
devices~\cite{Daraeizadeh2020,Xu2024}, with donor qubits addressed only
for device tuning rather than gate synthesis~\cite{Severin2025}, and GAA qubits not yet studied with any machine-learning approach (confirmed via systematic literature search through 2025).
Fragmented simulators hinder reproducibility because underlying noise models, Hamiltonian parameterisations, and interface conventions differ across tools, making cross-technology comparison unreliable~\cite{Krantz2019,Krantz2019b}. For instance, QuTiP~\cite{Johansson2013} lacks hardware-calibrated silicon noise profiles; Qiskit Aer~\cite{Qiskit2023} does not expose exchange or charge-noise channels; and no existing open-source framework provides a Gymnasium-compatible interface for all three silicon spin qubit families simultaneously.

\subsection{SiliQun: A Unified, Algorithm-Agnostic Platform}
\label{subsec:siliqun_overview}

We present \textbf{SiliQun~v5}, a simulation platform that addresses this
fragmentation by providing a single, unified Lindblad density-matrix engine
with calibrated hardware profiles for all three silicon spin qubit
technologies.
The central design principle of SiliQun is \emph{hardware neutrality}: the
physics engine is parameterised entirely by the \texttt{FirmwareProfile}
object, which encodes the noise characteristics of a specific hardware
technology.
Exchanging the profile alters the simulated physics without modifying the
Gymnasium interface, the action space, or the reward function.
Algorithms trained on one profile generalise to others by changing a single constructor argument; Section~\ref{sec:experiments} validates this claim empirically: the same DQN agent trained on the SiMOS profile achieves $\bar{F} > 0.97$ when evaluated on the donor and GAA profiles with no retraining (see Table~\ref{tab:cross_tech}).

This design enables a rigorous empirical test of simulator neutrality: if
the same algorithm, trained with the same hyperparameters, converges to
near-identical best-case fidelity on SiMOS, donor, and GAA profiles, then
the simulator is not artificially favouring any one technology.
Section~\ref{sec:experiments} reports exactly this test.

\subsection{Limitations of Existing Frameworks}
\label{subsec:intro_limitations}

No existing open-source framework satisfies the requirements for
multi-technology silicon spin qubit simulation with a standard RL interface.
QuTiP~\cite{Lambert2024} provides a comprehensive Lindblad solver but
offers no Gymnasium interface and covers no silicon-specific hardware
profiles.
Qiskit Pulse~\cite{Alexander2020} provides pulse-level control for IBM
superconducting hardware but has no silicon spin qubit device model.
C3~\cite{Wittler2021} supports closed-loop optimal control via GRAPE and
CMA-ES but does not implement standard RL algorithms.
TensorFlow Quantum~\cite{Broughton2020} supports hybrid quantum-classical
ML via Cirq integration but targets gate-model circuits rather than
device-level spin qubit physics.
Gymnasium-compatible RL environments for quantum circuit synthesis
(qgym~\cite{VanDerLinde2023}, Qiskit Gym~\cite{QiskitGym2024}) employ
abstract depolarising noise rather than detailed Lindblad dynamics derived
from device physics, making them unsuitable for technology-specific policy
training on silicon spin qubits.
Critically, none of these frameworks provides hardware profiles for donor
or GAA qubits, making cross-technology comparison impossible.

\subsection{Contributions}
\label{subsec:contributions}

We contribute the following:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Multi-technology hardware profiles:} Calibrated
    \texttt{FirmwareProfile} presets for SiMOS quantum dots, donor
    electron qubits, donor nuclear spin qubits, and GAA nanowire qubits,
    with noise parameters drawn from peer-reviewed experimental
    characterisations~\cite{Xue2022,Madzik2022,Tanamoto2025}.

  \item \textbf{SiliQun physics engine:} A Lindblad density-matrix
    simulator validated against three analytical benchmarks (Rabi
    oscillations, $T_1$ amplitude decay, Bell state fidelity) at machine
    precision ($\max\,\delta \leq 2.78 \times 10^{-16}$), with a
    93$\times$ GPU speedup over CPU-only simulation (see Section~\ref{subsec:gpu_benchmark}), enabling practical RL training throughput.

  \item \textbf{PGIRS methodology:} A five-phase engineering discipline
    (Noise-First Design, Gymnasium Compliance, Reward Engineering,
    Hierarchical Decomposition, Empirical Validation) that governs the
    design, implementation, and validation of physics-grounded RL
    simulation platforms.

  \item \textbf{Replication of Daraeizadeh et al.\ 2020 (SiMOS):}
    Faithful reproduction of the DQL and SARSA CZ gate-synthesis
    experiment~\cite{Daraeizadeh2020} on SiliQun's SiMOS profile,
    achieving $F = 0.9997$ versus the published $F > 0.999$ ($\Delta F <
    0.03\%$), validating the SiMOS noise model against an independent
    ground truth.

  \item \textbf{Replication of He et al.\ 2021 (Donor):}
    Faithful reproduction of the DQN universal state-preparation
    experiment~\cite{He2021} on SiliQun's donor electron profile,
    achieving best-case $F = 1.0000$ and mean cardinal-state fidelity
    $\bar{F} = 0.9748 \pm 0.0098$, validating the donor physics model.

  \item \textbf{First ML investigation on GAA silicon spin qubits:}
    Application of the DQN USP protocol to SiliQun's GAA profile,
    achieving best-case $F = 0.9999$ and mean cardinal-state fidelity
    $\bar{F} \approx 0.988$, establishing---to our knowledge---the first
    machine-learning benchmark for GAA silicon spin qubits.

  \item \textbf{Qiskit BackendV2 provider:} A pip-installable package
    (\texttt{siliqun-provider}) that registers SiliQun as a Qiskit
    \texttt{BackendV2}-compatible backend, enabling immediate integration
    with the Qiskit ecosystem and standard \texttt{transpile()} workflows.
\end{enumerate}
