\subsection{Cross-Algorithm Benchmark}
\label{subsec:cross_algo}
%% ----------------------------------------------------------

SiliQun's architecture deliberately supports diverse optimisation approaches through a unified physics engine and Gymnasium interface. Our experiments (\S\ref{subsec:r1_results}--\S\ref{subsec:e4_results}) rigorously test this algorithm-agnostic claim by evaluating seven approaches across five learning paradigms. Table~\ref{tab:cross_algo} consolidates these results, offering direct evidence of SiliQun's hardware-neutral capabilities.

\textbf{Algorithm families.}
We examine five distinct learning paradigms:
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{tabular on-policy} (SARSA)
\item \emph{deep value-based} (DQL, DQN)
\item \emph{deep policy-gradient} (PPO)
\item \emph{off-policy actor-critic} (SAC with and without stabilisation modules, TQC)
\item \emph{model-based quantum optimal control} (GRAPE, CRAB)
\end{itemize}
The model-based GRAPE and CRAB methods serve as privileged baselines with full Hamiltonian access, contrasting sharply with our model-free RL approaches. Crucially, we enforced identical environmental conditions for all algorithms, eliminating any potential bias from reward modifications or physics model adjustments.

\textbf{Results.}
Examining gate fidelity metrics in Table~\ref{tab:cross_algo} reveals striking patterns. The policy-gradient and actor-critic methods (PPO, SAC+modules, TQC) demonstrate particularly robust performance, maintaining $\bar{F} \geq 0.90$ across diverse tasks. Despite operating on different hardware profiles—TQC on GaAs (\texttt{gaas\_singlet\_triplet}) versus PPO/SAC+modules on SiMOS (\texttt{simos\_nominal})—all three achieve excellent fidelity, confirming SiliQun's capacity to support gradient-based methods across physically distinct systems. PPO's perfect mean fidelity ($\bar{F} = 1.0000$) for CZ synthesis suggests exceptional convergence, though we caution that simulated perfection doesn't guarantee hardware performance. SAC+modules comes remarkably close with $\bar{F} = 0.9995 \pm 0.0001$.

Value-based methods show more varied outcomes. DQL excels with $\bar{F} = 0.9998$ for CZ synthesis, proving SiliQun's effectiveness for discrete-action algorithms. DQN's performance on the demanding universal state preparation task (\texttt{donor\_electron} profile) proves more nuanced—while the mean fidelity drops to $\bar{F} = 0.8749 \pm 0.0466$, four of five seeds achieve $F \geq 0.99$ in their best evaluation. This spread likely reflects the USP task's inherent complexity combined with the donor-electron profile's unique noise characteristics, rather than fundamental limitations of value-based approaches.

SARSA's weak showing ($\bar{F} = 0.2500$) aligns with known limitations of tabular methods in continuous spaces, where $\varepsilon$-greedy exploration deteriorates. Though expected, this result provides valuable empirical confirmation rather than indicating interface shortcomings.

The oracle methods GRAPE and CRAB unsurprisingly achieve perfect fidelity ($\bar{F} = 1.0000$) in noiseless conditions (Table~\ref{tab:cross_algo}, footnote $^\dagger$). Under full SiMOS noise, GRAPE maintains $F = 0.9995 \pm 0.0001$ while CRAB reaches $F = 0.9991 \pm 0.0001$ (detailed in \S\ref{subsec:baselines}). Remarkably, our SAC+modules agent matches GRAPE's noisy-regime performance without any Hamiltonian knowledge.

\textbf{Significance.}
These results strongly validate SiliQun's algorithm-agnostic design. Seven distinct approaches—spanning fundamentally different learning paradigms—successfully interfaced with the same physics backend without modification. The observed fidelity variations authentically reflect algorithmic characteristics rather than simulation artefacts, precisely because SiliQun maintains rigorous consistency across its interface and physics engine.

The dramatic performance gap between our enhanced SAC+modules ($\bar{F} = 0.9995$) and standard SAC ($\bar{F} = 0.7518$) in identical conditions highlights how algorithmic refinements impact quantum control. This makes SiliQun particularly valuable for future algorithm development. TQC's high variance ($\bar{F} = 0.9036 \pm 0.0914$) offers equally important insights, revealing the GaAs singlet-triplet landscape's non-convexity and suggesting ensemble training or checkpoint initialisation may be necessary for robust convergence.
